# Mathematical Object Origin Archive | Hamiltonian Floer Homology

## 1. Archive Information

- Standard Name: Hamiltonian Floer homology
- Mathematical Field: Symplectic Geometry
- Abstract: Hamiltonian Floer homology is a homology theory generated by contractible one-periodic trajectories of a Hamiltonian system and defined by counting solutions of an elliptic partial differential equation connecting those trajectories. It was formed to turn the variational structure behind the Arnold fixed-point problem into a workable infinite-dimensional analogue of Morse homology [1–4].

## 2. Core Record

### Precise Description

Let \((M^{2n},\omega)\) be a closed symplectic manifold and, for the simplest classical construction, assume
\[
[\omega]|_{\pi_2(M)}=0,\qquad c_1(TM)|_{\pi_2(M)}=0.
\]
Let \(H:S^1\times M\to\mathbb R\) be a smooth Hamiltonian whose one-periodic orbits are nondegenerate. With the convention \(\iota_{X_{H_t}}\omega=-dH_t\), every fixed point \(p\) of the time-one Hamiltonian diffeomorphism \(\phi_H^1\) determines, and is determined by, the one-periodic orbit
\[
x_p(t)=\phi_H^t(p),\qquad \dot x_p(t)=X_{H_t}(x_p(t)).
\]
Write \(\mathcal P_0(H)\) for the subset of these orbits that are contractible. On the component \(\mathcal L_0M\) of contractible loops, the action functional is
\[
\mathcal A_H(x)=-\int_{D^2}\bar x^*\omega+\int_0^1 H_t(x(t))\,dt,
\]
where \(\bar x\) caps \(x\); the condition on \([\omega]\) makes its value independent of the cap. Its critical points are exactly the elements of \(\mathcal P_0(H)\).

Choose a regular family of \(\omega\)-compatible almost-complex structures \(J_t\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), the Floer chain group \(CF_*(H,J)\) is freely generated by \(\mathcal P_0(H)\) and graded by the Conley–Zehnder index. Its differential is
\[
\partial x_-=
\sum_{\mu_{CZ}(x_-)-\mu_{CZ}(x_+)=1}
\#_2\mathcal M(x_-,x_+)\,x_+,
\]
where \(\mathcal M(x_-,x_+)\) is the zero-dimensional moduli space, after quotienting by translation in \(s\), of finite-energy maps \(u:\mathbb R\times S^1\to M\) satisfying
\[
\partial_su+J_t(u)\bigl(\partial_tu-X_{H_t}(u)\bigr)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}u(s,\cdot)=x_\pm.
\]
Compactification of one-dimensional moduli spaces has broken trajectories as boundary; under the assumptions above, nonconstant holomorphic-sphere bubbling does not occur. Consequently \(\partial^2=0\), and
\[
HF_*(H)=H_*(CF_*(H,J),\partial).
\]
Continuation maps make this homology independent, up to canonical isomorphism, of the regular auxiliary choices. In this closed aspherical setting it agrees with \(H_*(M;\mathbb F_2)\), up to the conventional uniform grading shift [2–5].

### Mathematical Context and Formation

The concrete problem was the nondegenerate Arnold fixed-point conjecture. In the coefficient version relevant here, if a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism \(\phi\) of a closed symplectic manifold has only nondegenerate fixed points, one seeks to prove
\[
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)\geq \sum_k \dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_k(M;\mathbb F_2).
\]
A fixed point of \(\phi_H^1\) is a one-periodic solution of Hamilton's equation, and a contractible such solution is a critical point of \(\mathcal A_H\). This suggests applying Morse inequalities to \(\mathcal A_H\), so that topology would force many periodic orbits. The obstruction is that \(\mathcal A_H\) lives on an infinite-dimensional loop space and is strongly indefinite: its Hessian has infinitely many positive and infinitely many negative directions. Thus its critical points do not have the finite Morse indices needed for an ordinary Morse complex, and direct finite-dimensional Morse theory does not produce the desired comparison between orbit count and the homology of \(M\). In addition, a trajectory-counting theory must control noncompact families of connecting maps and possible holomorphic-sphere bubbling.

The formative insight was to retain the Morse-complex pattern while replacing its unavailable pieces by symplectic ones. The Conley–Zehnder index supplies a relative grading for periodic orbits and, under the stated condition on \(c_1(TM)\), a cap-independent integer grading in the contractible sector [1]. After choosing \(J_t\), the formal negative-gradient equation for \(\mathcal A_H\) becomes the elliptic Floer equation displayed above; its finite-energy solutions serve as connecting trajectories [2]. Gromov's pseudoholomorphic-curve compactness supplies the relevant compactness framework [6], while the condition on \([\omega]\) excludes nonconstant holomorphic spheres and hence sphere bubbles. Counting isolated solutions therefore converts the variational fixed-point problem into a chain complex rather than attempting to minimize the indefinite action directly. This is the mathematical mechanism by which Hamiltonian Floer homology was formed in response to the Arnold problem [2–4].

### Essential Role

Hamiltonian Floer homology makes the topological lower bound in the Arnold problem accessible. Its chain generators are precisely the contractible one-periodic Hamiltonian orbits. Therefore, for nondegenerate \(H\),
\[
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\phi_H^1)
\geq \#\mathcal P_0(H)
=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}CF_*(H,J).
\]
Its differential records the cancellations permitted by Floer trajectories, and continuation invariance permits comparison with a sufficiently small autonomous Morse Hamiltonian. In that small-Hamiltonian case the contractible Floer complex is identified, with a grading shift, with a Morse complex of \(M\); hence
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}HF_*(H)
=\sum_k\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_k(M;\mathbb F_2).
\]
Since the dimension of the homology of a finite-dimensional chain complex cannot exceed the dimension of its chain group,
\[
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\phi_H^1)
\geq \#\mathcal P_0(H)
\geq \sum_k\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_k(M;\mathbb F_2),
\]
which is the desired coefficient-field Arnold bound in the stated setting.

Each defining feature addresses a specific obstruction. Contractible periodic orbits as generators preserve a subset of the fixed points that must be counted; the Conley–Zehnder index replaces the unavailable finite Morse index; the elliptic trajectory equation and pseudoholomorphic compactness make connecting-orbit counts analyzable; and continuation maps separate the resulting homology from the chosen Hamiltonian while retaining its orbits at chain level. The construction therefore reformulates a dynamical fixed-point count as the comparison of a Hamiltonian-dependent chain complex with a Hamiltonian-independent topological invariant. This structural bridge, rather than Floer homology's later applications, is its direct contribution to the motivating problem [2–5].

## 3. Notes

“Floer homology” names a family of distinct theories. Hamiltonian Floer homology is the periodic-orbit theory described here; it should not be conflated with Lagrangian, instanton, monopole, or Heegaard Floer homology. On general closed symplectic manifolds, capped orbits, Novikov coefficients, and additional machinery for bubbling and transversality are required; the aspherical model above isolates the fixed-point mechanism without claiming to define every generalization [5]. Working over \(\mathbb F_2\) avoids orientation choices; versions over other coefficient rings require appropriate coherent-orientation data.
