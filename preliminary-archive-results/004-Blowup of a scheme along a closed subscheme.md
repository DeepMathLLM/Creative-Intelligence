# Mathematical Object Origin Archive | Blowup of a Scheme Along a Closed Subscheme

## 1. Archive Information

- Standard Name: Blowup of a scheme along a closed subscheme
- Mathematical Field: Algebraic Geometry
- Abstract: Given a closed subscheme \(Z\subset X\), the blowup is the canonical projective modification of \(X\) on which the ideal of \(Z\) becomes an invertible ideal, hence the ideal of an effective Cartier divisor. It is formed from the Rees algebra. For a chosen system of projective coordinates with common-zero scheme \(Z\), this principalization turns the coordinate ideal into a line bundle generated by those coordinates, so that their displayed rational map extends to a morphism. Non-Cartier base behavior is thereby replaced by compatible projective ratio data; a removable Cartier fixed factor, by contrast, produces no nontrivial modification.

## 2. Core Record

### Precise Description

Let \(X\) be a scheme and let \(\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X\) be a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf of finite type defining a closed subscheme \(Z\). Its Rees algebra is
\[
\mathcal R(\mathcal I)=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\mathcal I^n,
\qquad \deg(\mathcal I^n)=n.
\]
The **blowup of \(X\) along \(Z\)** is
\[
\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)
 =\operatorname{Proj}_X\mathcal R(\mathcal I),
\]
together with the structural morphism
\[
b:\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)\longrightarrow X.
\]
The morphism \(b\) is projective and is an isomorphism over \(X\setminus Z\). The scheme-theoretic inverse image \(E=b^{-1}(Z)\) is an effective Cartier divisor, called the exceptional divisor, and
\[
\mathcal I\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)}
 \cong \mathcal O_{\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)}(1)
 \cong \mathcal O_{\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)}(-E)
\]
with the relative-\(\operatorname{Proj}\) convention used here [1].

If \(X=\operatorname{Spec}A\) and \(I=(f_0,\ldots,f_r)\), then
\[
\operatorname{Bl}_I(X)=\operatorname{Proj}A[It],
\qquad
A[It]=A\oplus It\oplus I^2t^2\oplus\cdots\subseteq A[t].
\]
The standard chart \(D_+(f_it)\) has coordinate ring
\[
\bigl(A[It]_{f_it}\bigr)_0.
\]
When \(A\) is a domain and \(f_i\ne0\), this ring is the subalgebra \(A[f_0/f_i,\ldots,f_r/f_i]\) of \(\operatorname{Frac}(A)\). Thus the charts encode ratios without requiring any one \(f_i\) to be nonzero at the original center.

The defining universal property is principalization: if \(g:Y\to X\) is a morphism for which \(\mathcal I\mathcal O_Y\) is the ideal sheaf of an effective Cartier divisor, then there is a unique \(X\)-morphism \(Y\to\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)\) [1]. This condition is stronger than saying that the ideal is merely generated by one element: a principal generator that is a zero divisor does not define an effective Cartier divisor.

### Mathematical Context and Formation

A concrete motivating problem is the elimination of the base scheme of a chosen projective coordinate system. Let \(X=\operatorname{Spec}A\) be an integral affine variety and let nonzero functions \(f_0,\ldots,f_r\) generate \(I\). They give a morphism on
\[
U=\bigcup_iD(f_i)=X\setminus V(I),
\qquad
\phi_U:U\longrightarrow\mathbf P^r,
\qquad
x\longmapsto[f_0(x):\cdots:f_r(x)].
\]
The problem is to construct, canonically from this coordinate system, a projective modification \(b:X'\to X\) that is unchanged over \(U\) and on which \(\phi_U\) extends.

At a point of \(V(I)\), the images of all \(f_i\) in the residue field vanish, so the displayed tuple supplies no projective point. Algebraically, the generators do surject onto the ideal \(I\), but \(I\) need not be an invertible rank-one locally free sheaf. It therefore cannot yet serve as the line bundle quotient of \(\mathcal O_X^{r+1}\) required to define a morphism to \(\mathbf P^r\). Choosing a single denominator only produces the partial ratios \(f_j/f_i\), and these separate affine descriptions cannot be extended across the center unless their common factor can be made locally invertible.

This issue must be distinguished from a removable fixed factor. The common-zero scheme of a selected tuple need not equal the maximal indeterminacy locus of the associated rational map. For example, \((x,xy)\) displays a map on \(D(x)\), but after canceling \(x\) it extends as \([1:y]\). In sheaf terms, the ideal \((x)\) is already an invertible effective-Cartier ideal, and its blowup is the identity. The genuine obstruction addressed by a nontrivial blowup is thus not common vanishing by itself, but failure of the coordinate ideal to be invertible.

The Rees algebra supplies the missing construction. It retains every power \(I^n\), so multiplication records all compatibility relations among the ratios. Taking \(\operatorname{Proj}\) removes common scaling, while on each standard chart one generator of the pulled-back ideal becomes a local generator and the quotients \(f_j/f_i\) become regular. For integral \(X\), the resulting closed immersion into \(X\times\mathbf P^r\) identifies \(\operatorname{Bl}_I(X)\) with the scheme-theoretic closure of the graph of \(\phi_U\) [1][2]. This links the algebraic insight—replace \(I\) by its graded powers—to the geometric requirement of adjoining only compatible limiting projective ratios.

The map
\[
\mathbf A^2\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^1,
\qquad (x,y)\longmapsto[x:y],
\]
shows the non-Cartier obstruction explicitly. Here \(I=(x,y)\) is not invertible at the origin. The blowup is the closed subscheme of \(\mathbf A^2\times\mathbf P^1\), with projective coordinates \([U:V]\), defined by
\[
xV-yU=0.
\]
On \(U\ne0\), setting \(s=V/U\) gives \(y=xs\); on \(V\ne0\), setting \(t=U/V\) gives \(x=yt\). Above the origin, \([U:V]\) ranges through an exceptional \(\mathbf P^1\), recording the possible ratios, and projection to \([U:V]\) extends the original map.

### Essential Role

On \(B=\operatorname{Bl}_Z(X)\), the pulled-back coordinate ideal
\[
I\mathcal O_B\cong\mathcal O_B(-E)
\]
is invertible, and the pulled-back generators give a surjection
\[
\mathcal O_B^{r+1}\twoheadrightarrow I\mathcal O_B.
\]
An invertible quotient generated by \(r+1\) sections defines a morphism
\[
\widetilde\phi:B\longrightarrow\mathbf P^r
\]
whose restriction over \(U\) is \(\phi_U\). Thus the exact part of the motivating problem made tractable is extension of the chosen coordinate presentation: failure of \(I\) to be a line bundle is replaced by an invertible quotient, and the Rees relations ensure that the locally regular ratios glue.

Where \(I\) is genuinely noninvertible, the exceptional fiber can retain more than one compatible direction, as the \(\mathbf P^1\) over the origin does for \((x,y)\). It is not correct to assert this at every point of every center. If \(I\) is already the ideal of an effective Cartier divisor, the universal property makes \(B\cong X\); the construction then performs no geometric replacement and corresponds to cancellation of an invertible fixed factor.

The deeper structural contribution is canonicity and minimality for this principalization requirement. Every morphism \(Y\to X\) on which \(I\mathcal O_Y\) becomes an effective-Cartier ideal factors uniquely through \(B\) [1]. The blowup therefore does not choose arbitrary values at common zeros. It reformulates the obstruction as invertibility of the base ideal and supplies the universal projective space over \(X\) where that obstruction has been removed. This direct role is distinct from later uses of blowups in desingularization, intersection theory, and birational classification.

## 3. Notes

The blowup, its exceptional divisor, and the projectivized normal cone are related but distinct. One has
\[
E\cong\operatorname{Proj}_Z
\left(\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\mathcal I^n/\mathcal I^{n+1}\right).
\]
If \(Z\hookrightarrow X\) is a regular immersion, this is the projective bundle associated with the normal bundle, subject to the chosen convention for projectivization. A blowup need not make the modified space nonsingular. Also, resolving the base ideal of a selected coordinate system is stronger data than merely extending the corresponding rational map to its maximal domain: removable common Cartier factors explain why those two tasks can differ.
