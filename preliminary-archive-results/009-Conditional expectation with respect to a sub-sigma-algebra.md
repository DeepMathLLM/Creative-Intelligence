# Mathematical Object Origin Archive | Conditional expectation with respect to a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra

## 1. Archive Information

- Standard Name: Conditional expectation with respect to a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra
- Mathematical Field: Probability Theory and Stochastic Processes
- Abstract: For an integrable random variable \(X\) and a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal G\) representing available information, the conditional expectation \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\) is the almost-surely unique \(\mathcal G\)-measurable random variable whose integral agrees with that of \(X\) on every event in \(\mathcal G\). It resolves the problem of defining information-restricted averages when conditioning events need not form a positive-probability partition and pointwise ratio formulas are unavailable.

## 2. Core Record

### Precise Description

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\) be a probability space, let \(\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F\) be a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra, and let \(X\in L^1(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\). A **conditional expectation of \(X\) given \(\mathcal G\)** is a \(\mathcal G\)-measurable integrable random variable \(Y\) such that
\[
\int_G Y\,d\mathbb P=\int_G X\,d\mathbb P
\qquad\text{for every }G\in\mathcal G.
\]
It is denoted by \(Y=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\). It exists and is unique up to \(\mathbb P\)-almost-sure equality [1,2].

Existence follows by applying the Radon–Nikodym theorem to the finite signed measure
\[
\nu(G)=\int_G X\,d\mathbb P,\qquad G\in\mathcal G,
\]
which is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mathbb P\vert_{\mathcal G}\); the derivative \(d\nu/d(\mathbb P\vert_{\mathcal G})\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\). Equivalently, for an event \(A\in\mathcal F\), \(\mathbb P(A\mid\mathcal G)=\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_A\mid\mathcal G]\). When \(X\in L^2\), conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection of \(X\) onto the closed subspace \(L^2(\mathcal G)\) [1,2].

### Mathematical Context and Formation

The motivating problem is to assign an expected value to \(X\) using only information encoded by \(\mathcal G\). For a finite or countable measurable partition \((B_i)\) with \(\mathbb P(B_i)>0\), the elementary answer is constant on each cell:
\[
Y(\omega)=\frac{\mathbb E[X\mathbf 1_{B_i}]}{\mathbb P(B_i)}
\quad\text{when }\omega\in B_i.
\]
This construction simultaneously records what can be distinguished from the available information and preserves the average of \(X\) on every union of partition cells.

That ratio construction is inadequate for a general information structure. A sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra need not be generated by countably many positive-probability atoms; its informative fibers may all have probability zero, as often happens when one conditions on a continuously distributed random variable. Expressions that divide by the probability of an individual fiber are then undefined. Nor can one assume the existence of densities with respect to Lebesgue measure on an auxiliary state space. The problem is therefore not merely to assign numbers to isolated conditioning events, but to construct one \(\mathcal G\)-measurable random variable that represents all averages observable through \(\mathcal G\).

The key reformulation replaces pointwise ratios by integral constraints. The assignment \(G\mapsto\mathbb E[X\mathbf 1_G]\) is a signed measure on \(\mathcal G\) absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mathbb P\vert_{\mathcal G}\). Its Radon–Nikodym derivative is consequently a \(\mathcal G\)-measurable representative of the desired information-restricted average. Thus the conditional expectation is formed by combining measurability, which enforces dependence only on available information, with preservation of integrals over all observable events, which retains exactly the relevant averaging data [1,2].

### Essential Role

Conditional expectation makes the general conditioning problem tractable by replacing an often impossible family of eventwise quotients with a single measure-theoretic object. Its defining identity guarantees that every \(\mathcal G\)-observable test event receives the correct aggregate contribution from \(X\), while \(\mathcal G\)-measurability ensures that no unavailable distinctions are introduced. Absolute continuity supplies existence through the Radon–Nikodym theorem, and almost-sure uniqueness treats differences on null sets as immaterial rather than demanding undefined values on zero-probability fibers.

These features directly recover and extend the elementary partition construction: on each positive-probability atom, the Radon–Nikodym derivative has the familiar conditional-average value, whereas in nonatomic settings it remains defined as an almost-sure equivalence class without requiring positive-probability atoms or a Lebesgue density. The same characterization also gives the consistency law
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\mid\mathcal H\right]
 =\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal H],
\qquad \mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G,
\]
which expresses coherent loss of information. In \(L^2\), the projection identity
\[
\mathbb E\bigl[(X-\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G])Z\bigr]=0
\quad\text{for every }Z\in L^2(\mathcal G)
\]
shows the deeper structural change: conditioning becomes best mean-square approximation by a random variable measurable with respect to the available information, rather than division by the probability of an event [1,2].

## 3. Notes

A conditional expectation is generally an equivalence class modulo almost-sure equality; a particular representative is called a version. Conditioning on a random variable \(Z\) means conditioning on \(\sigma(Z)\). This object should be distinguished from a regular conditional probability, which is a probability kernel and whose existence as a suitably pointwise object may require additional hypotheses on the measurable spaces. Conditional expectation with respect to a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra itself requires no such standard-Borel assumption for integrable \(X\).
