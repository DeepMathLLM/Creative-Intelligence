# Mathematical Object Origin Archive | Grothendieck Group of Coherent Sheaves

## 1. Archive Information

- Standard Name: Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves
- Mathematical Field: Algebraic Geometry; Algebraic K-Theory
- Abstract: For a noetherian scheme or algebraic variety \(X\), the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves is the abelian group generated by coherent sheaves, with every short exact sequence made into an additive relation. It arose in the reformulation of Riemann–Roch as a functorial theorem for proper morphisms: formal differences and exact-sequence relations make alternating sums of higher direct images into well-defined classes, while finite locally free resolutions on regular spaces connect those classes to Chern characters.

## 2. Core Record

### Precise Description

Let \(X\) be a noetherian scheme and let \(\operatorname{Coh}(X)\) be its abelian category of coherent \(\mathcal O_X\)-modules. The **Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves** is
\[
G_0(X)=K_0(\operatorname{Coh}(X))
 =\frac{\bigoplus_{[\mathcal F]\in \operatorname{Iso}(\operatorname{Coh}(X))}\mathbb Z[\mathcal F]}
 {\left\langle [\mathcal F]-[\mathcal F']-[\mathcal F'']:\ 0\to\mathcal F'\to\mathcal F\to\mathcal F''\to0\ \text{exact}\right\rangle}.
\]
Thus \([\mathcal F]=[\mathcal F']+[\mathcal F'']\) for every short exact sequence. Equivalently, \(G_0(X)\) is universal among abelian groups receiving an invariant of coherent sheaves that is additive on short exact sequences [3].

These relations imply that a bounded complex \(\mathcal F^\bullet\) has the Euler class
\[
[\mathcal F^\bullet]=\sum_i(-1)^i[\mathcal F^i]
                  =\sum_i(-1)^i[\mathcal H^i(\mathcal F^\bullet)]
\]
in \(G_0(X)\). In particular, when \(f:X\to Y\) is a proper morphism in a setting where the sheaves \(R^if_*\mathcal F\) are coherent and vanish for large \(i\),
\[
f_![\mathcal F]=\sum_{i\geq0}(-1)^i[R^if_*\mathcal F]
\]
defines a homomorphism \(G_0(X)\to G_0(Y)\): the long exact sequence of higher direct images makes this expression respect the defining relations [2][4].

### Mathematical Context and Formation

The motivating problem was to turn Riemann–Roch from a numerical formula on one space into a theorem compatible with proper morphisms. Classical Riemann–Roch computes an Euler characteristic such as
\[
\chi(X,\mathcal F)=\sum_i(-1)^i\dim H^i(X,\mathcal F),
\]
and the desired relative form had to compare the cohomological direct image of \(\mathcal F\) under \(f:X\to Y\) with an intersection-theoretic pushforward on \(Y\). In a standard smooth form, the target identity is
\[
\operatorname{ch}(f_!\alpha)\operatorname{td}(T_Y)
 = f_*\bigl(\operatorname{ch}(\alpha)\operatorname{td}(T_X)\bigr).
\]
Grothendieck's classes of sheaves and their use in this problem were presented in the 1957 work later published in SGA 6 and in Borel and Serre's 1958 exposition [1][2].

A direct statement for individual sheaves could not express the left side. The ordinary direct-image functor \(f_*\) is only left exact, so a short exact sequence of coherent sheaves generally produces a long exact sequence of the derived images \(R^if_*\), not a short exact sequence after applying \(f_*\). Consequently the relative cohomological output is the alternating expression \(\sum_i(-1)^i[R^if_*\mathcal F]\), which is usually not represented by one sheaf. Isomorphism classes or the direct-sum monoid of sheaves provide no subtraction, and relations coming only from direct-sum decompositions do not force additivity for nonsplit short exact sequences. Rank, determinant, and individual Chern classes are likewise selected invariants, not a common domain on which the whole alternating derived image is defined.

The decisive formation step was therefore to replace a sheaf by a formal class, adjoin differences, and impose precisely the relations dictated by short exact sequences. Because consecutive terms of a long exact sequence cancel in the resulting group, the entire family \(R^if_*\mathcal F\) acquires one well-defined class \(f_![\mathcal F]\). On a regular scheme with the appropriate resolution property, a coherent sheaf has a finite locally free resolution, and its class becomes
\[
[\mathcal F]=\sum_j(-1)^j[E_j].
\]
This connects the coherent-sheaf class needed for direct images to vector bundles, on which the Chern character and Todd operations can be constructed [1][2]. The construction was thus tailored to the exactness defect that obstructed a functorial Riemann–Roch statement, rather than being merely a later bookkeeping device.

### Essential Role

The Grothendieck group made the cohomological side of relative Riemann–Roch tractable. Its formal negatives allow a derived direct image to be represented by an alternating sum, and its exact-sequence relations ensure that this sum is independent of decompositions and additive in \(\mathcal F\). For the structure morphism \(p:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k\), the identification \(G_0(k)\cong\mathbb Z\) by dimension sends
\[
p_![\mathcal F]=\sum_i(-1)^i[H^i(X,\mathcal F)]
\]
to \(\chi(X,\mathcal F)\); the old numerical invariant is therefore the point-valued case of the same pushforward mechanism.

On regular spaces, the passage through finite locally free resolutions lets \(\operatorname{ch}\) act on these classes: exactness makes the alternating class of a resolution independent of the resolution, while additivity of \(\operatorname{ch}\) turns it into a well-defined class in the rational Chow ring or cohomology. Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch can then compare two actual homomorphisms—\(f_!\) on sheaf classes and \(f_*\) on cycle classes—with the Todd class measuring their discrepancy [1][2]. The direct contribution of \(G_0(X)\) was not the proof of that comparison by itself; it was to supply the exact-sequence-additive domain in which the nonexact functor \(f_*\), its higher derived images, and characteristic classes could participate in one functorial formula.

Structurally, the object recast Riemann–Roch as a statement about universal additive classes rather than a collection of formulas for separate bundles or Euler characteristics. The later identification
\[
G_0(X)\cong K_0\bigl(D^b(\operatorname{Coh}(X))\bigr)
\]
expresses the same mechanism as a decategorification of bounded complexes: a complex and its cohomology determine the same Euler class [3].

## 3. Notes

Modern notation is not uniform. The coherent-sheaf group is written \(G_0(X)\), \(K'_0(X)\), or historically \(K(X)\). It should be distinguished on a singular scheme from \(K_0(X)\) formed from vector bundles or perfect complexes. There is a natural map \(K_0(X)\to G_0(X)\), which is an isomorphism under regularity and suitable finiteness/resolution hypotheses but not in general [3]. Also, tensor product does not make \(G_0(X)\) a ring for an arbitrary singular scheme, because tensoring coherent sheaves need not preserve exact sequences; its natural functorial feature relevant here is proper pushforward.
