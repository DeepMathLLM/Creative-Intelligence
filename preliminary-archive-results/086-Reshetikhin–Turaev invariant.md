# Mathematical Object Origin Archive | Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant

## 1. Archive Information

- Standard Name: Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant
- Mathematical Field: Knot Theory and Low-Dimensional Topology; Quantum Topology
- Abstract: The Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant is a scalar invariant of closed oriented three-manifolds constructed from the colored framed-link calculus of a modular tensor category. It arose from the problem of converting quantum-group link invariants, together with surgery presentations of three-manifolds, into quantities independent of the nonunique surgery link. Its defining Kirby-color sum and signature normalization are designed precisely to remove the two ambiguities represented by Kirby moves.

## 2. Core Record

### Precise Description

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a modular tensor category over a field such as \(\mathbb C\), and let \(I\) index representatives \(V_i\) of its simple objects. Write \(d_i\) for the quantum dimension of \(V_i\), \(\theta_i\) for its twist eigenvalue, and set
\[
\Omega=\sum_{i\in I}d_iV_i,\qquad
D^2=\sum_{i\in I}d_i^2,\qquad
p^{\pm}=\sum_{i\in I}\theta_i^{\pm1}d_i^2.
\]
Here \(\Omega\), the *Kirby color*, is a formal linear combination: coloring a component by \(\Omega\) means summing the colored-link evaluation over all simple labels with coefficients \(d_i\). Choose a square root \(D\) and put \(\kappa=p^+/D\). Modularity gives \(p^+p^-=D^2\).

Let \(L\subset S^3\) be an \(m\)-component framed link whose surgery produces a closed oriented three-manifold \(M_L\). Let \(F_{\mathcal C}(L;\Omega)\) be the scalar obtained from the Reshetikhin–Turaev ribbon functor after every component is colored by \(\Omega\), and let \(\sigma(L)\) be the signature of the linking matrix of \(L\). One common normalization, characterized by \(\tau_{\mathcal C}(S^3)=1\), is
\[
\tau_{\mathcal C}(M_L)
   =D^{-m}\kappa^{-\sigma(L)}F_{\mathcal C}(L;\Omega).
\]
The ribbon-category relations make \(F_{\mathcal C}\) invariant under isotopy of colored framed links. The Kirby-color sliding identity gives invariance under handle slides. Adding a disjoint \(+1\)-framed or \(-1\)-framed unknot multiplies the link evaluation by \(p^+\) or \(p^-\), respectively; the simultaneous changes in \(m\) and \(\sigma(L)\), together with \(p^+p^-=D^2\), are exactly canceled by the displayed normalization. Kirby's theorem therefore makes \(\tau_{\mathcal C}(M_L)\) independent of the surgery presentation. Variants in the literature can differ by choices of square roots, anomaly data, orientation conventions, or normalization of the value on \(S^3\).

### Mathematical Context and Formation

The motivating problem was to construct rigorous, computable quantum invariants of closed oriented three-manifolds from the newly available quantum-group invariants of knots, links, tangles, and ribbon graphs. The link theory already supplied highly structured scalars and operators: crossings were interpreted by braidings or \(R\)-matrices, twists recorded framing, and cups and caps were handled by duality. Reshetikhin and Turaev organized these data as a functor from colored ribbon graphs to representations of a quantum group [1]. This solved the local diagrammatic problem of producing framed-link invariants, but a closed three-manifold is not itself a link.

Surgery supplies the bridge. The Lickorish–Wallace theorem represents every closed connected oriented three-manifold by surgery on a framed link in \(S^3\). The decisive obstruction is that this presentation is very nonunique: Kirby calculus says that two surgery links represent the same oriented three-manifold exactly when they are connected by framed isotopy and Kirby moves, generated by handle sliding and addition or removal of a separated \(\pm1\)-framed unknot [2]. Consequently, evaluating an ordinary colored-link invariant on an arbitrarily chosen surgery link does not define a three-manifold invariant. A fixed label generally changes under a handle slide, while stabilization introduces a nontrivial framing-dependent scalar.

The construction was formed by matching categorical structure to these two failures. Instead of assigning one simple object to each surgery component, all simple labels are combined in the dimension-weighted Kirby color \(\Omega\). The fusion, duality, and braiding identities of the modular category then give \(\Omega\) an absorption or sliding property suited to handle slides. Stabilization cannot be eliminated by that sum alone, because the two possible framings evaluate to the Gauss sums \(p^+\) and \(p^-\). Recording the number of link components and the signature of the linking matrix supplies a compensating global factor. Thus the nonuniqueness theorem for surgery presentations did not merely test the invariant after its construction: its two moves dictated the two characteristic ingredients of the object. The resulting three-manifold invariant was established by Reshetikhin and Turaev using link polynomials and quantum groups [3], providing a mathematical construction in the direction of the three-dimensional quantum invariants proposed through Chern–Simons theory [4].

### Essential Role

The Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant made tractable the exact descent problem
\[
\{\text{framed surgery links in }S^3\}
   \longrightarrow
\{\text{closed oriented three-manifolds}\}:
\]
how can a quantum link evaluation be made constant on every Kirby-equivalence class? Its answer separates the obstruction according to the generators of that equivalence.

For a handle slide, the Kirby color \(\Omega=\sum_i d_iV_i\) replaces an arbitrary label by the categorical analogue of an integral: sliding another strand over an \(\Omega\)-colored component leaves the required total evaluation unchanged. The quantum-dimension weights are essential to this identity; an unweighted sum or a single color would not generally descend through handle slides. For blow-up or blow-down, the raw invariant acquires \(p^+\) or \(p^-\), but \(D^{-m}\kappa^{-\sigma(L)}\) changes by the reciprocal factor. The normalized scalar therefore depends on the manifold rather than on its chosen surgery diagram.

This mechanism also reformulated a global topological classification difficulty into finite algebraic data. Instead of comparing arbitrary triangulations or decompositions of a three-manifold directly, one computes a finite sum of ribbon-diagram evaluations and verifies invariance against two local presentation moves. The deeper structural viewpoint is that the braiding, twist, duality, semisimplicity, and nondegeneracy assembled in a modular category are exactly coherent enough to turn two-dimensional link diagrams into three-dimensional topological information. Extensions to topological quantum field theory and later applications are important consequences, but the object's direct originating role was the production of a presentation-independent three-manifold invariant from quantum link data.

## 3. Notes

The Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant is distinct from the Turaev–Viro invariant. The former is naturally built from a modular category and a surgery-link evaluation; the latter is a state sum from triangulation data and a spherical fusion category. Under appropriate hypotheses they are related—for example through Drinfeld centers—but they are not the same construction.

The phrase “Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant” is also used for the colored framed-link and ribbon-graph evaluations underlying the construction. In this archive it denotes the resulting invariant \(\tau_{\mathcal C}(M)\) of closed oriented three-manifolds, with the underlying link functor written \(F_{\mathcal C}\).
